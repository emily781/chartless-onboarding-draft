Chartless Practice Transcript

A fictional nutrition follow-up visit, written so you can see what Chartless does with a full session.

How to use it

  1. Click Copy the transcript below.
  2. In Chartless, open the client called Test.
  3. Choose Add Transcript and paste it in.
  4. Give it a few minutes, then read what it wrote.

You will get the clinical note, the client summary, goals and habits, supplement recommendations, and a draft Follow-up. Nothing here belongs to a real person.

0:00
RD: Hi Dana, good to see you. How have you been?

CLIENT: Good, mostly. Busy. Work has been a lot since the reorg, but
I have been sticking with things better than I expected.

RD: That is good to hear, and honestly the busy stretches are the
real test. Anything you want to make sure we cover today? I want to
get to your labs, but let us get your list down first.

CLIENT: The bloating, mainly. It is better but not gone. And I want
to know if I still need all these supplements, because it is a lot
of pills and a lot of money.

RD: Both good topics. Bloating and supplement pruning, and I will
add the lab review and your plan for the next twelve weeks. That is
our agenda.

1:12
RD: Let us start with how you have actually been feeling, before we
look at any numbers. Energy?

CLIENT: Better. The three o'clock crash is mostly gone. I used to
need coffee at two just to get through to dinner, and I have not
done that in maybe a month.

RD: That is meaningful. What about mornings?

CLIENT: Mornings are still hard, but I think that is sleep, not
food. I have been getting maybe six hours.

RD: We will come back to sleep. Hair, nails, anything like that?

CLIENT: The shedding stopped. I noticed that maybe six weeks ago,
there was much less in the drain.

RD: Good. Hold that thought, because I think your ferritin is going
to explain it.

2:30
RD: How about the gut? You said bloating is better but not gone.

CLIENT: Better. It used to be every night, really uncomfortable by
eight o'clock, like I looked pregnant. Now it is maybe two or three
nights a week and it is milder.

RD: What are those nights? Anything in common?

CLIENT: I have thought about that. I think it is when I eat out. Or
beans. I did the lentil soup thing you suggested and that was rough
the first couple of times.

RD: That is useful and it is a normal response. We increased your
fiber quite a lot quite quickly. Legumes are fermentable, so gas is
the expected consequence, not a sign of damage. How was it by the
third or fourth time?

CLIENT: Better, actually. Yes.

RD: That is adaptation, and it is why I want to keep them in rather
than pull them out. Bowel movements?

CLIENT: Every day now. That is new. It used to be every two or three
days.

RD: That is a real change. And straining, or urgency?

CLIENT: No. Normal, I would say.

4:05
RD: Let us do a typical day of eating. Walk me through yesterday.

CLIENT: Breakfast was the Greek yogurt with berries and the walnuts.
That has become automatic, I do it without thinking now.

RD: How much yogurt?

CLIENT: The container, the single serve. Two per cent.

RD: So roughly seventeen or eighteen grams of protein, and you are
getting some fat with the walnuts. Good. Lunch?

CLIENT: Salad with chicken from the place near the office. Or
leftovers if I have them. Yesterday was the salad.

RD: Dressing?

CLIENT: Whatever they put on it. Balsamic, I think.

RD: Fine. Afternoon?

CLIENT: The apple and peanut butter, most days. That was the thing
that fixed the crash, I think.

RD: I think so too. Dinner?

CLIENT: Yesterday was salmon and roasted broccoli and rice. That is
a good night. A bad night is pasta, or we order in.

RD: How often is a bad night?

CLIENT: Twice a week, maybe three.

RD: And after dinner?

CLIENT: Sometimes chocolate. Not every night.

6:20
RD: I want to say something about the pasta nights, because I do not
want you to hear this as a problem. Twice a week is completely
reasonable. What matters is the construction of the plate. If it is
pasta with a bit of sauce and nothing else, your blood sugar does
one thing. If it is the same pasta with chicken or beans in it and a
side of vegetables, it does something quite different.

CLIENT: So I do not have to cut it out.

RD: You do not, and I would rather you did not. Restriction has a
poor track record over a two year horizon. Construction beats
elimination. Same food, more protein and fiber next to it.

CLIENT: That is doable.

7:40
RD: Alcohol?

CLIENT: Wine on the weekends. One or two glasses Friday and
Saturday.

RD: Water?

CLIENT: Not enough, I know. Maybe three glasses plus coffee.

RD: Let us note that. Given where your fiber is now, water actually
matters more than it did before. Fiber without fluid is how people
end up more constipated, not less.

8:15
RD: Movement?

CLIENT: Walking, thirty minutes most days. I started the resistance
bands twice a week like we talked about, but I am not consistent.

RD: How many weeks out of the last twelve did the bands happen?

CLIENT: Honestly? Maybe five.

RD: Five out of twelve is not nothing. I want to protect that,
because muscle is doing more for your blood sugar than almost
anything else on this list. We will make it easier rather than
bigger.

9:30
RD: Sleep, you said six hours.

CLIENT: Six, sometimes six and a half. I fall asleep fine, I wake up
at three or four and then I am thinking about work.

RD: Every night?

CLIENT: Most.

RD: I am going to flag that as a real finding rather than a lifestyle
detail. Short sleep raises insulin resistance measurably, it raises
cortisol, and it drives appetite for exactly the foods you are
working to moderate. We can do everything right nutritionally and
sleep will still work against us. Is your primary aware?

CLIENT: I mentioned it once. It did not go anywhere.

RD: I would like you to raise it again, specifically the three a.m.
waking, and I will put it in my note to them.

11:00
RD: Let us get into the labs. I have the full panel from
endocrinology, drawn eleven days ago, fasting. I am going to go
through them in groups, and I will tell you both the standard range
and the functional range, because they are not the same thing and
the difference matters here.

CLIENT: What is the difference again?

RD: The standard range is built from the population that gets tested
at that lab. It is wide, and it is designed to catch disease.
Functional ranges are narrower and describe where people tend to
feel and function well. You can be inside the standard range, get a
clean bill of health, and still feel terrible. That is most of what
we are going to find today.

12:15
RD: Glycemic markers first, and this is your good news. Hemoglobin
A1C is 5.4. Twelve weeks ago you were 5.9.

CLIENT: Oh, that is a big drop.

RD: It is. 5.7 is the line for prediabetes, so you have moved from
inside that range to outside it. And A1C is a three month average,
so this is not a good week, it is a good quarter.

CLIENT: I did not expect that much.

RD: Fasting glucose is 91, was 104. Fasting insulin is 5.1, was
11.3. That last one is the number I care most about. Insulin is the
hormone that moves sugar out of the blood and into the cell. Yours
has more than halved, which means your cells are responding to a far
smaller signal than they were.

CLIENT: Is there a target?

RD: Under five is where I like to see it, so you are effectively
there. Your HOMA-IR, which is a calculation from the glucose and the
insulin together, is 1.1. Under 1.5 is good insulin sensitivity. You
were at 2.9.

14:40
CLIENT: Can you explain the A1C and the insulin again? I mix them
up.

RD: Sure, and it is worth understanding because these two tell you
different things. Think of A1C as the movie and glucose as the
photograph. Glucose is where your blood sugar was in the moment they
drew it. A1C is roughly the last three months averaged, because
sugar sticks to your red blood cells and they live about that long.

CLIENT: Okay.

RD: Insulin is different again. It is not the sugar, it is the key
that unlocks the cell to let the sugar in. Here is why it matters.
Insulin rises years before glucose does. Your body compensates by
making more and more insulin to keep glucose looking normal. So
someone can have perfect glucose and perfect A1C, and a fasting
insulin of eighteen, and be well down the road toward diabetes with
nothing on the standard panel to show for it. That is why I always
want insulin drawn.

CLIENT: Nobody has ever explained it that way.

RD: The short version: A1C tells you where you have been, insulin
tells you where you are heading. Yours are both good now.

17:00
RD: Lipids next. Total cholesterol 198. LDL 112. HDL 58.
Triglycerides 88.

CLIENT: My last doctor was worried about the LDL.

RD: I want to show you something more useful than the LDL on its
own. Your triglyceride to HDL ratio is 88 divided by 58, which is
1.5. Under two is where we want it, and it is one of the better
signals we have for the kind of small dense LDL particle that
actually drives risk. Yours is good. Twelve weeks ago your
triglycerides were 156 and your HDL was 49, which put you at 3.2.

CLIENT: So that improved too.

RD: Substantially, and triglycerides are the most diet responsive
lipid there is. That is the reduced added sugar and the alcohol
moderation showing up.

18:45
RD: Thyroid. This is the panel I want to spend some time on because
of the Hashimoto. TSH is 2.4. Standard range at this lab is 0.45 to
4.5, so you look normal. The functional range is 1.0 to 2.0, so you
are just above where I would like.

CLIENT: Is that bad?

RD: Not bad. Worth watching. Free T4 is 1.1, functional range 1.1 to
1.5, so you are at the bottom of it. Free T3 is 2.8, functional
range 3.0 to 4.0, so you are just under. Free T3 is the active
hormone, the one that actually does the work in your tissues.

CLIENT: And my dose has not changed.

RD: Correct, 88 micrograms of levothyroxine. What I am seeing is a
pattern rather than a problem: TSH drifting up, free T4 at the
floor, free T3 slightly under. That combination sometimes means the
dose needs revisiting, and sometimes it means conversion from T4 to
T3 is sluggish. Conversion needs selenium, zinc and iron, and we are
about to talk about your iron.

CLIENT: They are related?

RD: They can be. Low ferritin impairs the enzyme that converts T4 to
T3. So we may be looking at one problem wearing two hats.

21:10
RD: Your TPO antibodies are 210, down from 340.

CLIENT: Is that good? I never know what to do with that number.

RD: Lower is better, and the direction is right. Those antibodies
are the autoimmune activity against your thyroid. They fluctuate, so
I do not want to over read a single drop, but a hundred and thirty
point fall over twelve weeks alongside everything else improving is
encouraging.

22:20
RD: Now the iron studies, and this is the main event. Ferritin is
34. Twelve weeks ago it was 19.

CLIENT: So it went up.

RD: It nearly doubled, which tells me the iron bisglycinate is
working and you have been taking it. But the standard range starts
around 15, so the lab flags this as normal, and it is not where you
need to be. The functional range for energy, cognition and hair is
70 to 100.

CLIENT: So I am still less than half of where I should be.

RD: Yes. Ferritin is stored iron, the reserve tank. Serum iron is 78
and TIBC is 340, which gives a transferrin saturation of 23 per
cent, so you have enough circulating iron to keep hemoglobin normal.
Your hemoglobin is 13.2. You are not anemic and never were.

CLIENT: Then why do I feel it?

RD: Because anemia is the last thing to happen, not the first. Your
body protects red blood cells before anything else, so it drains the
storage tank to keep hemoglobin normal. Meanwhile the tissues that
need iron for energy production, cognition and hair follicles are
running short. That is why you had the shedding and the afternoon
crash while every standard result said you were fine.

CLIENT: That is frustrating to hear, honestly.

RD: It is very common. And the shedding stopping at six weeks lines
up almost exactly with when ferritin would have crossed into the
high twenties.

25:30
RD: Keep the iron bisglycinate going, same dose, and we recheck in
twelve weeks. Ferritin moves slowly, roughly what you just did, so I
would expect somewhere in the fifties next time and we may need a
third round after that.

CLIENT: How are you taking it, is what I meant to ask. With food or
without?

RD: Away from food where you can tolerate it, with vitamin C or
orange juice, because vitamin C substantially improves absorption.
Keep it an hour clear of coffee and tea, because the polyphenols
bind it, and four hours clear of calcium. And it must be four hours
from your levothyroxine.

CLIENT: I have been taking them together.

RD: That is worth fixing today, and it may partly explain the
thyroid numbers we just looked at. Iron binds levothyroxine in the
gut, so you may not be absorbing the full dose. Levothyroxine first
thing on an empty stomach, iron in the afternoon or with lunch.

CLIENT: Nobody told me that.

RD: It is one of the most common interactions I see, and it is
completely fixable.

28:00
RD: Vitamin D is 38. Was 31. Sufficiency starts at 30, optimal is 40
to 60, so you are close.

CLIENT: I went up to 2000 like you said.

RD: Let us hold at 2000 through the winter and recheck. I would
rather creep up than overshoot.

RD: B12 is 604, which is comfortable. Folate is 14, good. Magnesium
is 2.0, and that is a poor test because most magnesium is
intracellular, so I read it alongside symptoms. Any cramping, or
restless legs?

CLIENT: The leg cramps at night stopped.

RD: Then the glycinate is doing its job. Keep it.

29:45
RD: Inflammation. hs-CRP is 1.1, down from 3.4.

CLIENT: What does that measure?

RD: General inflammatory load. Under one is optimal, one to three is
moderate, over three is elevated. You have gone from elevated to the
top of optimal. Homocysteine is 8.2, which is fine, under ten.
Uric acid 4.9, fine.

RD: Kidney and liver. Creatinine 0.8, eGFR over ninety, both normal.
ALT 22, AST 19, both normal, and the ALT was 41 twelve weeks ago.

CLIENT: Is that important?

RD: It is quietly one of the better results here. A mildly elevated
ALT in someone with insulin resistance usually reflects fat in the
liver. It coming down alongside the triglycerides and the insulin
suggests you are pulling fat out of the liver. That is a big deal
and nobody would have told you, because 41 is technically within
range.

32:00
RD: Complete blood count is unremarkable. White cells 6.1,
hemoglobin 13.2, hematocrit 39.8, platelets 265. MCV 88, which is
normal, and I mention it because iron deficiency will eventually
drive it down. Yours is holding.

32:50
RD: Let me summarize the labs before we build the plan, because that
was a lot.

Three things clearly improved: your glycemic markers are now normal
across the board, and that is the headline. Your triglycerides and
inflammation both came down substantially. And your liver enzymes
normalized.

Two things still need work. Ferritin is 34, moving in the right
direction, still functionally low, and that is the most likely
driver of any remaining fatigue. And your thyroid panel is drifting
in a direction I want endocrinology to look at, made harder to read
by the iron interaction we just found.

One thing to watch: vitamin D is close but not yet optimal.

CLIENT: That is clearer than what I got from the endo, honestly.

35:00
RD: Now, your supplements, because you asked about pruning. Read me
what you are taking.

CLIENT: Iron bisglycinate. Vitamin D, 2000. Magnesium glycinate at
night. A multivitamin. Fish oil. Probiotic. Turmeric. And the
L-glutamine, though I stopped that.

RD: Let us go through them. Iron, keep, essential, we discussed the
timing. Vitamin D, keep at 2000. Magnesium glycinate, keep, the
cramps resolving is your evidence.

RD: The multivitamin, I would drop. It is duplicating your D and
your magnesium, the iron in it is a form you absorb poorly, and it
is money for very little.

CLIENT: Good, that is one.

RD: Fish oil, keep, but check the label. What is the combined EPA
and DHA per serving, not the total fish oil?

CLIENT: I do not know, I would have to look.

RD: Have a look tonight. We want at least a thousand milligrams of
EPA plus DHA combined. Many one thousand milligram fish oil capsules
contain only about three hundred, and people take one thinking they
are covered.

CLIENT: I will check.

RD: The probiotic. Honest answer, I do not know if it is helping
you. Your gut symptoms improved, but so did your fiber, your meal
timing and your stress. I would stop it for six weeks and see
whether anything changes. If nothing does, that is thirty dollars a
month back.

CLIENT: I am happy to test that.

RD: Turmeric, your CRP is 1.1 and falling, so if you like it, keep
it, but it is optional now. L-glutamine, you stopped it and your gut
got better, so leave it stopped.

RD: That takes you from eight to five, six if the fish oil stays.

CLIENT: That is much better.

39:30
RD: The bloating, back to your first question. Two or three nights a
week, mostly eating out or legumes. My read is that the legume
component is adaptation and is already resolving. The eating out
component is more likely portion size, speed of eating, and possibly
the carbonation and the wine.

CLIENT: I do eat fast when we are out.

RD: Try this. When you eat out, put the fork down between bites for
the first ten minutes only. Not the whole meal, ten minutes. It
slows the whole meal without you having to think about it, and
aerophagia, swallowing air, is a genuinely underrated cause of
bloating.

CLIENT: That is easy.

RD: And keep the legumes. Three or four times a week, and the
adaptation continues. If you pull them out now you lose the progress
and the fiber.

41:15
RD: Weight, since you asked me to track it. You are 171 today. You
were 179 twelve weeks ago.

CLIENT: I thought it would be more.

RD: Eight pounds in twelve weeks is a sustainable rate, and I care
much more about the metabolic picture than the number. Your insulin
halved and your A1C dropped half a point. Those did not happen
because of eight pounds. They happened because of what you ate and
how you moved, and they would be worth having even if the scale had
not moved at all.

CLIENT: That is a helpful reframe.

42:30
RD: Your plan for the next twelve weeks. Five things.

One. Separate the iron and the levothyroxine. Thyroid first thing on
an empty stomach, iron with lunch and vitamin C. This is the most
important change today.

Two. Keep the iron bisglycinate at the current dose and recheck
ferritin in twelve weeks.

Three. Stop the multivitamin and the probiotic. Check the EPA and
DHA on your fish oil and tell me the number.

Four. Resistance bands twice a week, and we make it easier: fifteen
minutes, same two days each week, put it in the calendar.

Five. Raise the sleep with your primary, specifically the three a.m.
waking. I will send a note.

CLIENT: And the water.

RD: Yes, good. Add two more glasses a day. Six things.

44:00
RD: Labs I want before the next visit: ferritin, iron studies,
vitamin D, and a full thyroid panel with TSH, free T4 and free T3.
Endocrinology can order those, and I will send them my summary
today, including the iron and levothyroxine interaction, which I
think they will want to know about.

CLIENT: Should I go in before then?

RD: If the fatigue comes back or the hair shedding returns, yes,
sooner. Otherwise twelve weeks.

44:40
RD: Let us book. Four weeks for a check in, then the labs, then the
full review at twelve.

CLIENT: Four weeks works. Afternoons are better for me.

RD: I have Wednesday the fourteenth at two, or Thursday the
fifteenth at three thirty.

CLIENT: Wednesday at two.

RD: Booked. I will send the visit summary, the goals, the fish oil
guidance and the iron timing in writing this afternoon so you are
not relying on memory.

CLIENT: Thank you. This was really useful.

RD: You have done the work, Dana. Moving out of prediabetes in
twelve weeks is not a small thing, and halving your fasting insulin
is the part I would be most proud of. See you in four weeks.

45:00
End of session.
